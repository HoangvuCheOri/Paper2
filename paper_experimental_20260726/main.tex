\begin{abstract}
This paper presents the implementation and experimental evaluation of a
velocity-level Backstepping trajectory-tracking controller augmented with
bounded sliding-mode-inspired nonlinear compensation for a
differential-drive wheeled mobile robot. The low-cost platform combines an
STM32F411 wheel-speed controller, ESP32/ESP-NOW communication, ROS\,2
high-level control, and an overhead AprilTag camera fused with wheel odometry
through an Extended Kalman Filter. Backstepping, conventional SMC, and the
compensated controller are evaluated on circular, figure-eight, and square
references. The currently selected physical runs show comparable
Backstepping and BSMC performance on the circle and square, while the
selected pure-SMC square pilot exhibits larger corner transients. Manual
circle-displacement pilots further expose the importance of global
relocalization and inner wheel-speed behavior. Because the figure-eight,
square-SMC, and disturbance datasets are selected pilots rather than
balanced locked-gain repeats, they are reported as preliminary physical
evidence and not as final statistical validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Trajectory tracking of differential-drive robots is affected not only by the
nonholonomic kinematics but also by actuator deadzones, wheel asymmetry,
wireless timing variation, encoder quantization, and localization drift.
Backstepping provides a constructive nominal tracking law, whereas bounded
nonlinear compensation can increase robustness without discontinuous
switching. This work evaluates that combination on a low-cost physical
platform and compares it with independently configured Backstepping and SMC
baselines.

\section{Controller and Platform Summary}

For pose $q=[x,y,\theta]^\top$, the kinematic model is
\begin{equation}
  \dot{x}=v\cos\theta,\qquad
  \dot{y}=v\sin\theta,\qquad
  \dot{\theta}=\omega .
\end{equation}
Using body-frame errors $e_x$, $e_y$, and $e_\theta$, the compensated
commands are
\begin{align}
  v_{\rm cmd} &=
    v_d\cos e_\theta+k_1e_x+
    K_{s1}\operatorname{sat}(e_x/\varphi_1),\\
  \omega_{\rm cmd} &=
    \omega_d+v_d(k_2e_y+k_3\sin e_\theta)+
    K_{s2}\operatorname{sat}((e_\theta+ce_y)/\varphi_2).
\end{align}
The controller runs on a ROS\,2 host. Velocity commands and wheel telemetry
are exchanged through an ESP-NOW bridge, while the STM32 closes the two
wheel-speed loops. An overhead camera observes a 0.15-m AprilTag and supplies
absolute pose corrections to a custom EKF.

\section{Experimental Method}

The physical tests use circle, figure-eight, and 2-m square references.
Camera-derived tracking errors are computed after rigid local-frame
alignment, without temporal smoothing. Nominal experiments compare
Backstepping, BSMC, and SMC. Manual disturbance pilots are restricted to the
circle and use two displacement orientations. A run is considered recovered
only when its position error returns below the fixed threshold and remains
there for the prescribed hold interval.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

The selected physical runs confirm closed-loop operation of the complete
STM32F411--ESP-NOW--ROS\,2--AprilTag system. BSMC and Backstepping provide
similar performance on the selected circle and square runs; BSMC gives the
lowest selected square position RMSE, while Backstepping gives the lowest
circle RMSE and straight-square heading RMSE. The selected pure-SMC square
pilot is limited by large post-corner transients. Manual displacement tests
show that robust global relocalization and reliable inner wheel-speed
tracking are prerequisites for meaningful controller comparison.

The principal limitation is the unbalanced nature of the current pilot data.
Future work will freeze controller parameters, restore and verify the inner
wheel-speed loop, and collect equal-repeat validation runs under identical
firmware, localization, initial-pose, battery, and floor conditions. Only
that locked dataset should be used for mean--standard-deviation claims.
